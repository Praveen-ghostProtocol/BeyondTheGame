# A1 — Alaknanda Hostel field-study synthesis

**Project:** Beyond the Game  
**Study site:** Alaknanda Hostel, IIT Madras  
**Source:** Firsthand account of serving hostel sports secretary, supplied in project conversation; not independently audited.  
**Research status:** A1 initial account documented; further verification and measurements needed.  
**Study choice:** Alaknanda as primary site; other-hostel comparison later.

## 1. Scope and users

- Approximate residential population: **400 students** (user-reported estimate).
- Regular number of equipment users: **unknown**.
- Most popular sports, by sports secretary account: badminton, volleyball, football, cricket.
- Equipment categories reported: badminton rackets and shuttlecocks; cricket bats and balls; footballs; volleyballs; basketballs; table-tennis equipment; hockey equipment and additional sport categories. **Category counts and completeness have not been audited**. Do not infer possession of every Inter-IIT equipment category from a general claim.
- Dedicated sports room, accessed by a key held by the security guard, available **24/7** to hostel residents.
- Equipment stored in racks/shelves in principle; students do not consistently return items to designated locations. Secretary reports that locating equipment is generally not difficult despite disorganization.

## 2. Roles and responsibilities (reported)

| Role | Current tasks | Boundaries / vulnerabilities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Student | Request key; state intended equipment; sign register; select and return items; return key | Items and quantities taken/returned not physically verified; several students may enter on same key |
| Security guard | Hold student ID while key issued; record details and declared items; hand over and collect key; return ID | Does not check actual item movement or condition |
| Sports secretary | Plan purchases, assess damage when reported, coordinate repair/replacement and sports shop work | Typically discovers damage or missing equipment only after later demand/complaint; not involved in daily issuing |
| Hostel office | Approve/reimburse costs or pay suppliers according to reported procurement route | Detailed authorizations and policy documents not yet verified |
| External sports shops | Quote, supply equipment and repair certain items | Repair availability and turnaround not quantified |

## 3. Transaction process (reported)

1. Student approaches guard for sports-room key.
2. Guard retains student ID card and records name, roll number, room number, intended equipment, issue timestamp and student signature in paper register.
3. Guard gives sports-room key to student.
4. Student collects equipment independently; actual item type/count are not verified.
5. Multiple students may enter while the key is in circulation.
6. Student places equipment back, returns key, and signs return entry; guard records return timestamp and gives back the ID.
7. Return is accepted on trust, without item- or condition-level verification.

**Reported end-to-end duration:** approximately **five minutes** from starting trip to guard through collecting equipment; **informal estimate**, includes walking and register time, not an instrumented sample. Secretary says register writing can be somewhat slow. **No daily transaction count is known.** Weekends are believed busier, not measured.

## 4. Damage, repair and replacement (reported)

- Typical failures: cricket bats breaking; badminton racket stringing/net issues; table-tennis rubber coming loose. Footballs and volleyballs usually remain serviceable until ageing/replacement.
- Damaged items are **not systematically segregated** from usable items.
- Students generally do not proactively notify secretary; problems surface later when a needed item is absent/unusable or replacement is requested.
- Repairability assessed case by case. Where possible, items are taken to sports shops; otherwise replaced.
- **Reported purchasing procedure:** below ₹10,000: obtain quotation, pay and subsequently seek reimbursement; at or above ₹10,000: obtain quotations from three shops, select lowest, submit to hostel office, which makes direct payment. Reported processing approximately three working days, collection potentially about a week. **Treat monetary threshold and approvals as secretary's account until policy is checked.**
- Secretary estimates high purchasing workload around inter-hostel tournaments. No systematic purchase or repair database reported; office retains bills.

## 5. Concrete reported incident

A leather-ball English willow cricket bat, approximately ₹15,000, reportedly broke beyond repair in the previous year after unsuitable handling/preparation, requiring a cheaper replacement (reported approximate price ₹7,500–₹10,000) under budget constraints. The exact contributing cause, item specification, year, and purchase invoice have not been independently verified. This is **one specific reported case**, not a measured frequency of bat failure.

The secretary also mentioned analogous badminton/table-tennis problems but did not provide distinct incident details. **Do not fabricate additional cases**; they remain category-level reports until documented.

## 6. Current system characteristics

- Register has: name, roll number, room number, item description/intended quantity, issue time, return time and student signatures for issue and return. Guard signature was not reported.
- No spreadsheet, purpose-built application or formal item-level inventory tracking is used at the study site.
- No stated borrowing duration limits; loss/damage penalties are supposed to exist but enforcement and accountability are uncertain.
- No reported method for adjudicating a missing item that a borrower asserts was returned.
- Students may informally share available equipment on first-come-first-served basis.
- Secretary reports other IIT Madras hostels use broadly similar process; this is **unverified cross-hostel generalization**, not an audited finding.
- Photos and redacted supporting documentation may be available later; they have not been collected here.

## 7. Initial research finding

**Evidence-supported process observation:** The existing controls identify a person associated with the *room key* and record **declared** item movements. They do not verify physical equipment movements, condition, or subsequent person-to-person transfers.

**Hypotheses requiring tests:** This control gap may increase inventory discrepancies, reduce damage accountability and delay repair/replacement. It is not yet established that it causes significant borrowing delays or that digitization would improve it.

**Important counterevidence:** Secretary reports equipment can generally be found easily and student complaints are limited. Consequently, do **not** present long search delays or widespread dissatisfaction as proven findings.

## 8. Evidence still needed

1. Physical item counts/condition audit, by category, with safe anonymized item labels.
2. Redacted register format and sampled, anonymized transaction counts and times (with authorization).
3. At least a small measured sample of actual issue/return flow timing, distinguishing walking, register and collection components.
4. Reported bat breakage invoice or related non-personal evidence, if available.
5. Existing procurement rules or a sample redacted quotation, if available.
6. Role and policy confirmation from a guard or hostel-office representative, if feasible.

## 9. A1 conclusion and handoff

The primary **observed design gap** is lack of verifiable item-level custody and condition tracking, not proven retrieval slowness. Phase A2 should map issuance, return, handover between students, damage, repair, and purchasing as separate interconnected processes. Phase A3 must establish actual baseline metrics before claiming any quantified performance problem.
